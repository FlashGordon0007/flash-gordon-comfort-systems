How a tankless water heater actually works

A tank heater keeps 40–80 gallons of water hot 24/7, whether you’re using it or not. A tankless unit only fires when you open a tap. Cold water flows through a heat exchanger, the burner modulates, and you get a continuous stream of hot water at the set temperature.

That means no standby losses, no giant tank of hot water sitting there all day, and no “we ran out of hot water” moments when the second shower starts.

  • On‑demand: burner only runs when you open a hot water tap.
  • Endless hot water: properly sized units don’t “run out” like a tank.
  • Wall‑mounted: frees up floor space in closets, garages, and basements.

So… should you stay with a tank or go tankless?

Stay with a standard tank if:

  • • You’re selling the home soon and want the lowest upfront cost.
  • • You don’t mind giving up a closet or floor space.
  • • You’re okay with occasional “cold shower” moments when the tank runs out.

Go tankless if:

  • • You plan to stay in the home and want long‑term efficiency.
  • • You want to free up space in a closet, garage, or mechanical room.
  • • You have a busy household and never want to “run out” of hot water.
  • • You’re considering hydronic heating, radiant floors, or future upgrades.
